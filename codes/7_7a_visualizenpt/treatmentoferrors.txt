Implement a robust time-series error-analysis module for observables measured
along a molecular-dynamics or Markov-chain Monte Carlo trajectory.

CONTEXT

Given a stationary scalar time series

    A_0, A_1, ..., A_{N-1}

sampled at a constant interval dt, estimate:

1. The sample mean.
2. The autocovariance and normalized autocorrelation functions.
3. The integrated autocorrelation time.
4. The correlation-corrected standard error of the sample mean.
5. The effective number of independent samples.
6. Diagnostics indicating whether the trajectory is long enough for these
   estimates to be credible.

The implementation must not treat consecutive trajectory samples as
independent.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SAMPLE MEAN
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

For N samples, define

    mean_A = (1/N) * sum_i A_i

This is the estimator of the equilibrium expectation value <A>.

The sample mean is itself a random variable. Its uncertainty depends on the
time correlations in A.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. AUTOCOVARIANCE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The theoretical autocovariance at lag k is

    C(k) = <(A_i - <A>)(A_{i+k} - <A>)>

For finite data, replace the unknown theoretical mean with the sample mean:

    C_hat(k)
        = 1/(N-k) * sum_{i=0}^{N-k-1}
            (A_i - mean_A) * (A_{i+k} - mean_A)

for

    k = 0, 1, ..., N-1

In particular,

    C_hat(0)

is the estimated variance of the observable.

Use the lag-dependent denominator N-k, as shown above. Be explicit in the
documentation that this differs from estimators that divide every lag by N.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. NORMALIZED AUTOCORRELATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Define the normalized autocorrelation function

    rho_hat(k) = C_hat(k) / C_hat(0)

Therefore,

    rho_hat(0) = 1

Interpretation:

- Large positive rho_hat(k): samples k steps apart contain similar information.
- rho_hat(k) near zero: samples at that separation are approximately
  uncorrelated.
- Negative values are valid and must not automatically be discarded.
- At large lag, the estimator becomes very noisy because only N-k pairs remain.

Do not estimate the integrated autocorrelation time by summing rho_hat(k) over
all available lags. The long-lag noise can dominate the result.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. VARIANCE OF THE SAMPLE MEAN
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

For correlated samples,

    Var(mean_A)
        = 1/N^2 * sum_i sum_j Cov(A_i, A_j)

For a stationary time series this can be written as

    Var(mean_A)
        = 1/N * sum_{k=-(N-1)}^(N-1)
            (1 - |k|/N) C(|k|)

When N is much longer than the correlation time, approximate this by

    Var(mean_A)
        approximately
        C(0)/N * sum_{k=-infinity}^{infinity} rho(|k|)

Define the integrated autocorrelation time in units of samples as

    tau_int
        = (1/2) * sum_{k=-infinity}^{infinity} rho(|k|)
        = 1/2 + sum_{k=1}^{infinity} rho(k)

This gives

    Var(mean_A) approximately 2 * tau_int * C(0) / N

and therefore

    standard_error_mean
        = sqrt(2 * tau_int * C_hat(0) / N)

If the sampling interval is dt, then the physical integrated autocorrelation
time is

    tau_int_physical = tau_int * dt

Keep tau_int in sample units internally, and return the physical value
separately when dt is supplied.

For independent samples,

    rho(k > 0) = 0
    tau_int = 1/2

so the formula reduces to

    Var(mean_A) = C(0)/N

as required.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EFFECTIVE SAMPLE SIZE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Define the effective number of independent samples as

    N_eff = N / (2 * tau_int)

Then the standard error may also be expressed as

    standard_error_mean = sqrt(C_hat(0) / N_eff)

Interpret 2*tau_int as the efficiency loss relative to independent sampling.

Examples:

- tau_int = 0.5 gives N_eff = N, corresponding to independent samples.
- tau_int = 5 gives N_eff = N/10.
- tau_int = 50 gives N_eff = N/100.

Negative autocorrelation can produce tau_int below 0.5 in some processes.
Do not hard-code tau_int >= 0.5 unless that restriction is explicitly requested.
However, flag non-positive estimates as unreliable or pathological because they
would imply a non-positive estimated variance of the mean.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WINDOWED ESTIMATE OF INTEGRATED AUTOCORRELATION TIME
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The naive finite-data estimate

    tau_int_hat = 1/2 + sum_{k=1}^{N-1} rho_hat(k)

is generally poor because rho_hat(k) has high variance at large k.

Instead, truncate the sum at a window M:

    tau_int_hat(M)
        = 1/2 + sum_{k=1}^{M} rho_hat(k)

Choosing M creates a bias-variance tradeoff:

- M too small:
    The estimator is biased because genuine correlation is omitted.

- M too large:
    The estimator has excessive variance because noisy long-lag estimates are
    included.

Use the self-consistent rule from the notes:

    choose the smallest M such that

        M >= 10 * tau_int_hat(M)

Only accept a candidate M when tau_int_hat(M) is finite and positive.

Suggested procedure:

    cumulative_tau = 0.5

    for M from 1 to max_lag:
        cumulative_tau += rho_hat(M)

        if cumulative_tau > 0 and M >= 10 * cumulative_tau:
            selected_M = M
            tau_int = cumulative_tau
            stop

If no M satisfies the criterion, return the best available estimate but mark
the result as unconverged. Do not silently claim that the autocorrelation time
has been reliably estimated.

The default maximum lag should not exceed N-1. It may be sensible to use a
smaller configurable maximum lag for numerical and statistical stability.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. REQUIRED OUTPUTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Return a structured result containing at least:

    mean
    observable_variance
    standard_error
    tau_int_samples
    tau_int_physical
    effective_sample_size
    selected_window
    autocovariance
    autocorrelation
    converged
    warnings
    n_samples
    dt

Definitions:

    observable_variance = C_hat(0)

    standard_error
        = sqrt(2 * tau_int_samples * observable_variance / N)

    effective_sample_size
        = N / (2 * tau_int_samples)

The autocovariance and autocorrelation arrays only need to be returned up to
the maximum lag that was evaluated.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. BURN-IN
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Treat equilibration or burn-in separately from autocorrelation error analysis.

Integrated autocorrelation time measures correlations within the stationary
part of the trajectory. It does not determine whether the initial state was
drawn from equilibrium.

The API should therefore support an explicit burn_in argument:

    production_data = data[burn_in:]

All reported statistics should be computed from production_data only.

Do not claim that cancellation between positive and negative correlations
reduces the required burn-in period. The lecture notes explicitly separate
burn-in from the integrated-autocorrelation correction.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. DIAGNOSTICS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Generate warnings in at least the following cases:

1. Too few samples.
2. Zero or numerically negligible C_hat(0).
3. No self-consistent window was found.
4. The selected window is close to the maximum available lag.
5. N is not substantially larger than the estimated correlation scale.
6. N_eff is very small.
7. tau_int is non-finite or non-positive.
8. The time series contains NaN or infinite values.

A useful trajectory-length diagnostic is

    N / tau_int

or equivalently N_eff.

Do not present a strict universal convergence threshold unless configured by
the caller. Instead, return the ratio and warnings so that the user can judge
whether the simulation contains many correlation times.

The central self-consistency issue is:

- A short run produces a poor estimate of the autocorrelation time.
- A poor autocorrelation estimate may fail to reveal that the run is too short.
- A sufficiently long run should produce a stable autocorrelation estimate and
  thereby provide evidence of its own adequacy.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. COMPUTATIONAL IMPLEMENTATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Provide two possible autocovariance implementations:

A. Direct O(N * max_lag) implementation for testing and small arrays.

B. FFT-based O(N log N) implementation for long trajectories.

For an FFT implementation:

1. Convert input to a floating-point array.
2. Subtract the sample mean.
3. Zero-pad sufficiently to avoid circular wraparound.
4. Compute the power spectrum from the FFT.
5. Apply the inverse FFT to obtain the unnormalized autocovariance sums.
6. Divide lag k by N-k.
7. Normalize by C_hat(0) to obtain rho_hat(k).

Check the FFT result against the direct implementation in unit tests.

Do not confuse the following:

- C_hat(0), the variance of the observable.
- Var(mean_A), the variance of the estimated mean.
- tau_int, measured in sample intervals.
- tau_int * dt, measured in physical time.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. PROPOSED PYTHON API
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Use an interface resembling:

    @dataclass
    class TimeSeriesErrorResult:
        mean: float
        observable_variance: float
        standard_error: float
        tau_int_samples: float
        tau_int_physical: float | None
        effective_sample_size: float
        selected_window: int
        autocovariance: np.ndarray
        autocorrelation: np.ndarray
        converged: bool
        warnings: list[str]
        n_samples: int
        dt: float | None

    def analyze_time_series(
        values: ArrayLike,
        *,
        dt: float | None = None,
        burn_in: int = 0,
        max_lag: int | None = None,
        window_factor: float = 10.0,
        method: Literal["fft", "direct"] = "fft",
    ) -> TimeSeriesErrorResult:
        ...

Validate all inputs and include type hints and docstrings.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. UNIT TESTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Include tests using synthetic processes with known behavior.

Test 1: Independent Gaussian noise

Expected:

    rho(k > 0) approximately 0
    tau_int approximately 0.5
    N_eff approximately N
    standard_error approximately sample_std / sqrt(N)

Test 2: AR(1) process

    X_t = phi * X_{t-1} + epsilon_t

For |phi| < 1,

    rho(k) = phi^k

and the theoretical integrated autocorrelation time is

    tau_int
        = 1/2 + sum_{k=1}^{infinity} phi^k
        = (1 + phi) / (2 * (1 - phi))

Therefore,

    2 * tau_int = (1 + phi) / (1 - phi)

Test several values, such as phi = 0.2, 0.7, and 0.9. Use sufficiently long
series and statistical tolerances rather than exact equality.

Test 3: Negatively correlated AR(1)

Use a negative phi and verify that negative correlations are included rather
than clipped.

Test 4: Constant signal

C_hat(0) is zero. Return zero observable variance and an appropriate warning.
Do not divide by zero when constructing rho.

Test 5: Short trajectory

Verify that the result is marked unconverged when no self-consistent window can
be identified.

Test 6: FFT versus direct estimator

For the same input and maximum lag, the two implementations should agree
within floating-point tolerance.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. OPTIONAL PLOTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Provide an optional plotting function showing:

1. The time series.
2. rho_hat(k) versus lag.
3. The cumulative estimate

       tau_int_hat(M) = 1/2 + sum_{k=1}^{M} rho_hat(k)

4. The selected truncation window M.
5. The line M / window_factor, so the self-consistency intersection can be
   visually inspected.

Label lag both in sample units and physical units when dt is available.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not thin the trajectory merely to create apparently independent samples.
The error in the mean can be calculated using all samples and the integrated
autocorrelation time.

Positive and negative correlations both contribute to tau_int. Their partial
cancellation can make the estimator of the mean more efficient than a scheme
that waits until samples appear completely independent.

The final reported uncertainty must be correlation corrected:

    mean_A +/- standard_error_mean

where

    standard_error_mean
        = sqrt(2 * tau_int * C_hat(0) / N)

Do not report the independent-sample error C_hat(0)^(1/2)/sqrt(N) unless
tau_int is approximately 0.5.
